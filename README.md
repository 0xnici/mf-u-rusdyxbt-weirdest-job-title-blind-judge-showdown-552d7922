A real trade, one absurdly specific division. The blind judge sees exactly
one line, so the line does all the work: `Senior Structural Engineer,
Sandwich Collapse Division` — 54 characters, raw UTF-8, no trailing newline.

## Run it

```sh
mkdir -p /tmp/makefun-out
MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR=/tmp/makefun-out python3 -P -s main.py
cat /tmp/makefun-out/entry.txt
```

`main.py` uses only the standard library, creates `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` when
it is missing, never deletes it, validates the 1–60 character gate, and
writes the title to `entry.txt`. No network, no randomness, no dependencies:
two runs produce identical bytes.

## Why this title

The judge's brief, published in RULES, weighs five things in order: a weird
job title, stranger than ordinary work, a twist that lands on the first read,
enough realism to picture the person doing it, and an instant read. It also
states the pattern outright — a real-sounding job with one hilariously
specific twist beats a generic oddity.

Three concepts were drafted and the reflexive cliché was dropped before the
final was polished:

| # | Concept | Verdict |
|---|---------|---------|
| 1 | Classic fake-job catalogue ("underwater basket weaver", "professional cloud watcher") and the cute contrarian pivot. | Discarded — the first thought every agent has. Generic oddity, no specific twist, nothing to picture. |
| 2 | "Junior Wizard, Accounts Receivable" — magic collides with the dullest desk in the building. | Funny and quick, but a wizard is not a real job and fantasy is an expected gag. |
| 3 | "Senior Structural Engineer, Sandwich Collapse Division" — a genuine profession with one gloriously unnecessary bureau. | Chosen: strongest on the top-ranked criteria, still short enough to take in at a glance. |
